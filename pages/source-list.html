An experiment beside the enzyme and branch lists, which stay as they are. Here the top layer is the source fatty acid: each network is read from where it starts, and every name carries its series, so the 3-series reads EPA › PGH3 › PGE3 and not as a row inside PGE2. Three layers are shown: the network (the heading), the family group (numbered) and the item (a map), with the branch point as the item's prefix. The mediators of an item are its rows.

The list

Numbers: estimated processes, from the same tree as the branch pages, counted per fatty acid. File names: the group's number and the item (10-pg-pge2, 20-pg3). The first digit is the network: 1 AA, 2 EPA, 3 DHA, 4 the smaller sources.

Three networks, but of very different size: AA holds about half of the mediator processes, EPA and DHA a sixth to a fifth each. The smaller sources (DGLA, linoleic acid, mead acid) fill one map each. The receptors are counted in full once, with their first events.

What this layout gives, and what it costs

GivesCosts
The start is visible: AA › PGH2 › PGE2 and EPA › PGH3 › PGE3 are two items with their own names, not one item with a row. The enzymes repeat in every network: COX-1 and COX-2, mPGES-1, 5-LOX, LTA4H appear in the AA, EPA and DGLA maps. Checked once (the step is the same chemistry), drawn three times.
Each network is one connected map from its fatty acid, so the three can be the regions of the master map (rows of the floor plan). What the series share sits apart: competition at the same enzyme (EPA slowing AA's prostanoids, the effect of fish oil) is seen only in an assembly (99, Competition).
The n-3 products are shown in their own right: the EPA network has its own resolvins, the DHA network has no prostaglandins at all. The receptors leave the networks: one receptor takes PGE1, PGE2 and PGE3, so it is drawn once in 5x and each network's map ends at its ligand, linked to the receptor map.
Small series stop being tiny maps: the EPA prostanoids are one map with six rows (PGE3, PGD3, ...). The family view (all PGE together, as in the branch list) is an assembly here, made by script from the same pieces.

The claims on this page are from the literature as known; each gets its references when its item is drawn.
