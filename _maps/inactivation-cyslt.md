Leukotriene E4 is the end of the cysteinyl leukotriene chain and the form that appears in urine, where it is the measure of this branch in patients. Part of it is excreted unchanged (Sala 1990).

The rest is marked and shortened. N-acetylation is by NAT8, the acetyltransferase of the mercapturic acid pathway, shown directly with the human enzyme (Veiga-da-Cunha 2010). Carbon 20 is then hydroxylated and oxidised to the acid, and the shortening chain takes it from there, the one step here with human genetic proof.

No gene is drawn for the two omega-oxidation steps. The enzyme that hydroxylates leukotriene E4 is not the one that hydroxylates leukotriene B4: the two differ in kinetics and compete for different substrates (Orning 1987), and no CYP4F enzyme has ever been assayed on a cysteinyl leukotriene. The enzymology comes from rat liver, and the human evidence is at the level of the metabolites.

The N-acetyl branch is drawn as far as the hydroxylated form. In man it carries little flux: N-acetyl-leukotriene E4 is a minor urinary metabolite, although it is the main one in rat bile.

## What this map means

These are the steps a mediator takes after it has acted, the routes by which it leaves the network. They are not new branches of production. Where a product keeps its activity, this text says so, because the route alone does not tell whether a signal has ended.
