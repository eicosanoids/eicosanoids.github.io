Peroxynitrite, made where nitric oxide meets superoxide, nitrates prostacyclin synthase on tyrosine 430 and stops it (Schmidt 2003, Zou 1997). The reaction is catalysed by the enzyme's own heme-thiolate centre, which is why it happens at low concentrations of the oxidant and why ordinary antioxidants do not protect against it (Schildknecht 2013).

What makes this more than the loss of one enzyme is where the substrate then goes. PGH2 that is not turned into prostacyclin still acts, and it acts on the thromboxane receptor, so the same oxidative stress that removes a vasodilator leaves behind a signal that constricts vessels and activates platelets.

Thromboxane synthase has the same heme-thiolate fold and sits on the same substrate, and peroxynitrite does not nitrate it (Zou 1997). The map draws both branches for that reason.
