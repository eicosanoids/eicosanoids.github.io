PGF2α comes from PGH2 by reduction, through the aldo-keto reductases AKR1A1, AKR1B1 and AKR1C3 and the PGF synthase PRXL2B (AKR1A1 and AKR1B1 the most potent of the human aldo-keto reductases, Lacroix Pépin 2013), and from PGE2 through carbonyl reductase CBR1. HPGD oxidises PGF2α to 15-keto-PGF2α, which CBR1 can reduce back, and 15-keto-PGE2 gives 15-keto-PGF2α through CBR1 as well. The prostaglandin reductases PTGR1 and PTGR2 reduce 15-keto-PGF2α to its 13,14-dihydro form, and CBR1 reduces that to 13,14-dihydro-PGF2α.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. This is the branch with a drug class of its own: analogues of PGF2alpha acting on the FP receptor are first-line treatment for glaucoma, with darkening of the iris and the eyelashes as their mark (Alexander 2002). That the effect runs through the receptor and not somewhere else is shown by mice without it, in which the drug no longer lowers eye pressure (Crowston 2005).
