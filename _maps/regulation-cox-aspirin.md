Aspirin does not block the cyclooxygenases the way other inhibitors do. It acetylates a serine in the channel that leads to the active site, which stops COX-1 for the life of the enzyme and so stops thromboxane production in the platelet, which cannot make new protein. COX-2 is not stopped by the same acetylation. Its channel is wider, so arachidonate still reaches the iron, and the acetylated enzyme turns it into 15R-HETE instead of prostaglandin G2. That product is the start of the aspirin-triggered lipoxins and resolvins, which is why a drug that blocks prostaglandin production also starts a branch of its own.
