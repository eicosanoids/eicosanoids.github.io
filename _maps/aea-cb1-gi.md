Anandamide (N-arachidonoylethanolamine) was the first endogenous ligand found for the cannabinoid receptor (Devane 1992). At CB1 it is a partial agonist, weaker than 2-arachidonoylglycerol (Sugiura 1999), and the receptor acts through a Gi protein that inhibits adenylyl cyclase (Matsuda 1990). Anandamide is broken down by fatty acid amide hydrolase into arachidonic acid and ethanolamine. It also opens the ion channel TRPV1, and cyclooxygenase-2 oxygenates it to prostaglandin ethanolamides (prostamides).

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. This is the clearest clash in the atlas between human genetics and drugs. A woman with lifelong insensitivity to pain and high circulating anandamide carries a microdeletion in the FAAH pseudogene together with a weakened FAAH variant, which validates the enzyme as a pain target in people (Habib 2019). Yet an inhibitor that cut FAAH activity by more than 96 per cent gave no pain relief in knee osteoarthritis, and another compound of the same class caused an acute neurological syndrome with one death in a phase 1 trial (Huggins 2012, Kerbrat 2016).

The common FAAH variant that lowers the enzyme and raises anandamide is associated with addiction and, less consistently, with obesity-related traits (Flanagan 2006, Lopez-Cortes 2024). Cannabidiol, a weak inhibitor of anandamide breakdown, improved acute schizophrenia about as much as amisulpride, and the improvement followed the rise in serum anandamide (Leweke 2012).
