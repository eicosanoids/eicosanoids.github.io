The cyclooxygenases turn eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) into PGG3 and PGH3, and the same terminal synthases as for arachidonic acid make PGE3, PGD3, PGF3α, PGI3 and TXA3, which hydrolyses to TXB3. EPA is a poor substrate for COX-1, oxygenated at about a tenth of the rate of arachidonic acid, and COX-2 prefers arachidonic acid when both are present, so the 3-series is made in small amounts (Wada 2007). TXA3 acts on the thromboxane receptor about as strongly as TXA2 (Wada 2007). Its weak effect on platelets comes from how little is made. Prostacyclin I3 is formed in humans after EPA intake (Fischer 1987, Knapp 1986).

Predicted steps, drawn with dashed arrows: the reduction of 15-keto-PGE3 and the PGJ3 series. The enzymes and the non-enzymatic chemistry are those of the 2-series, and no paper measures them on the 3-series.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. The point of the 3-series is competition: EPA uses the same enzymes as arachidonic acid, so 5-lipoxygenase output moves from leukotriene B4 towards the much weaker leukotriene B5, and cyclooxygenase output from PGE2 towards PGE3, which binds the same receptors more weakly (Lee 1984, Hawcroft 2010). That is the mechanism behind fish oil, and it runs through the maps of this group.

The clinical record is mixed and worth reading before any claim is made from these maps. High-dose purified EPA cut ischaemic events by about a quarter in statin-treated patients with raised triglycerides, the only large positive trial of this kind, but its mineral oil comparator itself raised inflammatory markers, which leaves the size of the effect open (Bhatt 2019, Ridker 2022). Across seven trials, marine n-3 raised the risk of atrial fibrillation with the dose, most steeply above one gram a day (Gencer 2021).
