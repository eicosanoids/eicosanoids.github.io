Prostacyclin synthase (PTGIS) makes PGI2 from PGH2, mainly in the endothelium, and thromboxane synthase (TBXAS1) makes TXA2 and 12(S)-HHTrE, mainly in platelets. Both products are unstable in water. PGI2 hydrolyses to 6-keto-PGF1α, which gives 6-keto-PGE1 and the urinary 2,3-dinor-6-keto-PGF1α, and TXA2 hydrolyses to TXB2, whose 11-dehydro and 2,3-dinor forms are measured in urine. HPGD oxidises PGI2 to 15-oxo-PGI2. Acting on PGG2, the purified synthases give the 15-hydroperoxy forms of PGI2 and TXA2. In cells the cyclooxygenase reduces PGG2 to PGH2 first, so these forms are a minor side route. The cyclooxygenase step from PGG2 to PGH2, drawn in grey, belongs to the PGE2 map.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. The two mediators of this map pull against each other, and the balance is what matters. Prostacyclin is low in pulmonary arterial hypertension, and drugs along that pathway improve the course of the disease (Del Pozo 2017). Selective COX-2 inhibitors raise cardiovascular risk because they remove endothelial prostacyclin while leaving platelet thromboxane untouched, which took rofecoxib and its relatives off the market (Marwali 2006).

The third product of thromboxane synthase has a part in recovery rather than in damage: 12-HHT acting on BLT2 speeds the migration of keratinocytes and the closing of a wound, and aspirin delays wound healing by cutting it (Liu 2014).
