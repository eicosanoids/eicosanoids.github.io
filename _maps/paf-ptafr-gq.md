Platelet-activating factor (PAF, 1-O-alkyl-2-acetyl-sn-glycero-3-phosphocholine) is made in the same step that releases arachidonic acid: cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha cuts alkyl-arachidonoyl phosphatidylcholine into arachidonic acid and lyso-PAF, which an acetyltransferase turns into PAF. Its receptor PTAFR was cloned from guinea pig lung by its response in frog oocytes (Honda 1991) and then from human leukocytes (Kunz 1992). PTAFR works through Gq, phospholipase C, inositol trisphosphate and calcium, and also through other G proteins (Honda 2002). The calcium signal activates cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha again, so PAF and the eicosanoids made with it reinforce each other in mast cells, neutrophils, eosinophils and platelets.
