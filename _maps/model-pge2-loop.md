PGE2 made by a macrophage comes back to the same cell through its EP2 and EP4 receptors and raises cyclic AMP. Protein kinase A then phosphorylates CREB on serine 133, and CREB switches on the genes of the two enzymes that made the PGE2, COX-2 (PTGS2) and mPGES-1 (PTGES). In RAW 264.7 macrophages PGE2, dibutyryl cyclic AMP or forskolin alone induce both genes, through EP2, PKA and CREB (Díaz-Muñoz 2012, Hinz 2000). This map draws that loop as one module, drawn so that a Boolean model can be read from it.

## The loop

Lipopolysaccharide starts it. NF-κB binds the promoters of both genes (Díaz-Muñoz 2010), and the cyclic AMP response element, the NF-κB site and the NF-IL6 sites of the PTGS2 promoter can each drive it on their own (Mestre 2001). Each gene is therefore drawn with two transcription steps, one with CREB and one with NF-κB, and either is enough. The messenger RNAs are drawn because they carry the delay of the loop, and COX-2 protein is short-lived, degraded at the endoplasmic reticulum and by the proteasome (Chen 2013, Neuss 2007).

PGE2 leaves the cell through MRP4 (ABCC4, Reid 2003). Outside, it binds EP2 and EP4, which both stimulate adenylate cyclase. EP4 is desensitised within minutes, most likely by a G protein-coupled receptor kinase, while EP2 is not (Nishigaki 1996, Neuschäfer-Rube 1999). The prostaglandin transporter (SLCO2A1) takes PGE2 back up and 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase (HPGD) oxidises it (Nomura 2004). Phosphodiesterase 4B hydrolyses cyclic AMP, and lipopolysaccharide induces it in macrophages (Jin 2005), so the stimulus that starts the loop also brings a brake on it. Protein phosphatase 1 removes the phosphate from CREB and ends its activity (Hagiwara 1992).

## What the model says

Read as a Boolean model, the loop behaves in two phases. While lipopolysaccharide is present, NF-κB drives both genes and PGE2 is made and released, but phosphodiesterase 4B keeps cyclic AMP down, so the loop itself does not run. When the stimulus goes, the model has two outcomes. If cyclic AMP reaches CREB before the messenger RNAs fall, the loop holds itself on, carried by EP2, which does not desensitise. Otherwise everything switches off. Which outcome a cell takes depends on rates, which a Boolean model cannot weigh. Once the loop holds, nothing in the module switches it off. What ends it in a cell is clearance outpacing synthesis, the slow down-regulation of both receptors (Nishigaki 1996), or a stimulus that brings phosphodiesterase 4B back.

## Model check (draft)

The module was tested as a model-ready map should be: Boolean rules written by hand from the statements, the same map converted to CellDesigner and read by CaSQ, and the two compared node by node. Of 20 nodes, 14 get the same rule. The other six differ for three reasons. CaSQ builds a node's rule only from the steps that make it, so the uptake of PGE2 reads as a second source of PGE2 inside the cell, and PGE2 then stays on even with COX-2 blocked. For the same reason the hydrolysis of cyclic AMP and the desensitisation of EP4 do not reach the rules of what they remove. And with the dephosphorylation of CREB drawn, CaSQ keeps resting CREB as a variable that switches off at its first update, so CREB is never phosphorylated and the loop cannot close. The first point is also a point about the drawing. Newly made PGE2 leaves the cell unoxidised and only PGE2 taken back up reaches the dehydrogenase (Nomura 2005), so the two pools inside the cell are different species. This section is a draft and will change.

## Disputed

NF-κB on the PTGS2 promoter is drawn green. In RAW 264.7 cells the NF-κB site was not needed for induction by endotoxin, while the cyclic AMP response element and two NF-IL6 sites were (Wadleigh 2000). The module depends on that arc. Without it, lipopolysaccharide switches on only PTGES and no PGE2 is made, so the factor that carries the stimulus to PTGS2, NF-κB or NF-IL6, decides whether the loop starts.
