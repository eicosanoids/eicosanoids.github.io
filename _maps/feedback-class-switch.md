Early in acute inflammation neutrophils make LTB4 and prostaglandins, and later the same exudates make lipoxin A4, as the inflammation resolves. Human blood neutrophils exposed to PGE2 switch from LTB4 to lipoxin A4: PGE2 turns on the gene of 15-lipoxygenase, and the cell then oxygenates arachidonic acid at carbon 15 first, which 5-lipoxygenase finishes as lipoxin A4 (Levy 2001). Lipoxin A4 stops the further entry of neutrophils.

So a mediator of the first phase starts the second one. The receptor and the transcription factor that carry the PGE2 signal to the gene were not identified in that work, and the map draws PGE2 as a stimulation of the transcription step. The last step, from the epoxide to lipoxin A4, is drawn without an enzyme here. The lipoxin map gives its enzymes.
