12-lipoxygenase in macrophages, and 15-lipoxygenase, turn DHA into 14S-HpDHA and then into 13S,14S-epoxy-maresin (Serhan 2008). Macrophages open the epoxide to maresin 1 with an enzyme not yet identified, as LTA4 hydrolase does not convert it, and soluble epoxide hydrolase gives maresin 2 (Dalli 2013). Glutathione transferases conjugate the epoxide to MCTR1, which γ-glutamyl transferase and a dipeptidase trim to MCTR2 and MCTR3 (Dalli 2016). The lipoxygenases also make 11S-HpDHA.

Maresin 1 is drawn in green as contested: in human macrophages and in neutrophils with platelets, the peak at its place is a related 7,14-dihydroxy isomer, not maresin 1 itself, and the enzyme that would open the epoxide to it is unknown (Hofstetter 2026, Schebb 2026). In human platelets given DHA, MCTR1 forms even when LTC4 synthase is blocked, so another glutathione transferase makes it in cells, LTC4 synthase and GSTM4 are drawn from the recombinant enzymes (Liening 2019). Maresin 2 is found above the limit of quantification in neutrophils incubated with platelets and stays solid, as do the epoxide and its conjugates.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for recovery. Maresin 1 works through LGR6: mice without that receptor have worse type 2 inflammation and a higher viral load after respiratory syncytial virus (Krishnamoorthy 2023). Maresin 1 also blocks TRPV1 currents and relieves inflammatory and chemotherapy-induced pain, and it speeds tissue regeneration (Serhan 2012). In isolated human bronchi, protectin D1 and maresin 1 reduce the hyperreactivity that interleukin 13 produces (Abma 2026).

The dispute about whether these mediators are made at working concentrations in people is on the E-series resolvin page, and the contested steps here are drawn green.
