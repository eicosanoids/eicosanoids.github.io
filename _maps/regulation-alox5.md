Arachidonate 5-lipoxygenase (ALOX5) converts arachidonate, through 5-hydroperoxyeicosatetraenoic acid, into leukotriene A4, the precursor of all leukotrienes. The reaction takes place at the nuclear envelope, where ALOX5 binds its partner ALOX5AP (FLAP), helped by Ca2+. Leukotrienes are the 5-lipoxygenase branch of eicosanoid synthesis, beside the prostaglandins and thromboxane of the [eicosanoid maps](https://metabolismregulation.github.io/eicosanoids/). Zileuton inhibits ALOX5, and in mild to moderate asthma it reduced exacerbations that needed corticosteroids and improved lung function. Two phosphorylations act in opposite directions and also decide where ALOX5 is. MAPKAP kinase 2, activated by p38α (MAPK14) in stress signalling, phosphorylates ALOX5 at Ser272 in the nucleus, giving a form that stays there and is active without a Ca2+ signal. Protein kinase A, activated by cAMP, phosphorylates Ser524 in the cytosol, giving a form that cannot enter the nucleus and is inactive.
