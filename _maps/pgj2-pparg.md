Not every eicosanoid works through a receptor on the cell surface. 15-deoxy-delta12,14-PGJ2, the last product of the PGD2 branch, binds the nuclear receptor PPARgamma and turns on the genes it controls, which is how the prostanoid pathway reaches adipocyte development and the handling of lipid in macrophages (Forman 1995, Kliewer 1995). The ligand-bound receptor works as a pair with the retinoid X receptor alpha, and the pair sits on the response element of its target genes (Kliewer 1992). In human monocytes the pair turns on the scavenger receptor CD36 and with it the uptake of oxidised low-density lipoprotein, the step that makes a foam cell (Tontonoz 1998).

How much 15-deoxy-delta12,14-PGJ2 a cell actually makes is contested. Measured by mass spectrometry it is a minor product, its amount does not rise while preadipocytes become adipocytes, and it rises neither in the joint fluid of patients with arthritis nor in the urine after cyclooxygenase is switched on, so it may not be the ligand that activates this receptor in the body (Bell-Parikh 2003).

The map draws CD36 as the one target gene, as the example measured in human cells. It does not draw how the ligand reaches the nucleus.
