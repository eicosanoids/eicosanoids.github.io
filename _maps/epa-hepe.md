Platelet 12-lipoxygenase (ALOX12) turns EPA into 12(S)-HpEPE, and the two 15-lipoxygenases (ALOX15, ALOX15B) into 15(S)-HpEPE, both reduced to their hydroxy acids. ALOX15 gives a broader set of products than the other human lipoxygenases: 8,15-dihydroxy isomers from 15(S)-HpEPE and, through its hydroperoxide isomerase activity, epoxy alcohols, 13-hydroxy-14,15-epoxy-EPE from 15(S)-HpEPE and hepoxilin B4, the 10-hydroxy-11,12-epoxide, from 12(S)-HpEPE. Acting after 5-lipoxygenase, ALOX12 turns 5(S)-HEPE into 5S,12S-diHEPE, and 5-lipoxygenase turns 15(S)-HpEPE into lipoxin A5, made best when ALOX15B and 5-lipoxygenase act together (Kutzner 2020). Human neutrophils make lipoxin A5 when given 15-HEPE (Peh 2024).

GPX1 and GPX4 reduce the hydroperoxides of EPA, while GPX2 hardly does (Schwarz 2023). 5(S)-HpEPE and 5(S)-HEPE come from the 5-series leukotriene map and are drawn in grey.
