These acids are hydroxylated at the far end, saturated, or shortened. CYP4F3 hydroxylates 5-HETE and 12-HETE, and the same enzyme was shown to have no activity at all on 15-HETE (Kikuta 1998), so 15-HETE has only the shortening route. 5-Oxo-ETE is hydroxylated too, and this is one of the few products here that is genuinely much weaker than its parent (Powell 1996).

12-HHT, the third product of thromboxane synthase, is oxidised by 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase and then reduced by PTGR1. Both products keep full agonist activity at BLT2 (Yasukawa 2023), so this pair of steps changes the molecule without ending the signal.

20-HETE is oxidised to the dicarboxylic acid, by an alcohol dehydrogenase whose isoform is not identified. The product is a vasodilator and an agonist of PPARalpha and PPARgamma (Fang 2007), the opposite of what 20-HETE does, so this is a switch of activity and not a termination at all.

Where the species matters: the dihydro route of 12-HETE and of 12-oxo-ETE was measured in pig leukocytes, and the step to the dicarboxylic acid in mouse podocytes. The CYP4F3 reactions and the 12-HHT steps are human.

## What this map means

These are the steps a mediator takes after it has acted, the routes by which it leaves the network. They are not new branches of production. Where a product keeps its activity, this text says so, because the route alone does not tell whether a signal has ended.
