The metabolites of the pro-resolving mediators were found by mass spectrometry in human cells and in exudates, and not one of their enzymes has been assigned. Protectin D1 is hydroxylated at carbon 22, maresin 1 at carbon 22 and oxidised at carbon 14, and resolvin E1 goes three ways at once.

Most of these products are not inactive. 22-Hydroxy-protectin D1 is about as potent as protectin D1 itself (Tungen 2014), and 22-hydroxy-maresin 1 and 14-oxo-maresin 1 still raise phagocytosis by macrophages (Colas 2016). The resolvin E1 metabolites are the clearer case: the dihydro, the 19-hydroxy and the 20-carboxy forms are all weaker than resolvin E1 (Hong 2008).

Gaps worth knowing: maresin 2, resolvin E2 and resolvin E3 have no metabolome study at all, and no further step is known for the 22-hydroxy forms.

## What this map means

These are the steps a mediator takes after it has acted, the routes by which it leaves the network. They are not new branches of production. Where a product keeps its activity, this text says so, because the route alone does not tell whether a signal has ended.
