COX-1 and COX-2 do nearly the same chemistry, and the difference that matters for inflammation is how long they last. COX-2 carries a short tail that COX-1 does not, and a sugar put on asparagine 594 inside it marks the protein for the degradation route of the endoplasmic reticulum, so the enzyme lives hours where COX-1 lives a day or more (Mbonye 2006, Mbonye 2008). Mutating that asparagine stabilises COX-2, and putting the same tail into COX-1 destabilises it. A mouse carrying the shortened tail overexpresses the enzyme (Wada 2009).

The marked protein is pulled out of the membrane and ubiquitinated with the help of derlin-1, caveolin-1 and the p97 motor, and the proteasome finishes it (Chen 2013). A second set of ligases under the COP9 signalosome does the same work, and some coxibs speed it up (Neuss 2007). Caveolin-1 is named here and not drawn.

Nothing here is phosphorylation. No paper shows COX-2 itself being phosphorylated with an effect on what it does, although many describe kinases that change how much of it a cell makes.
