Lipoxins need two lipoxygenases, often in two cells. In one route 15-lipoxygenase makes 15(S)-HpETE or 15(S)-HETE and leukocyte 5-lipoxygenase adds the second oxygen. In the other, platelet 12-lipoxygenase turns leukotriene A4 from neutrophils into lipoxins. Both routes pass through the epoxide 5,6-Ep-15(S)-HETE to lipoxin A4 and B4. Aspirin-acetylated COX-2 makes 15(R)-HpETE, which 5-lipoxygenase turns into the aspirin-triggered 15-epi-lipoxins. HPGD inactivates lipoxin A4. Human eosinophils conjugate the lipoxin epoxide with glutathione to lipoxin C4, which is trimmed to lipoxins D4 and E4. These were identified once, by their chromatography and light absorption. How lipoxin C4 leaves the cell is not known, and its export is drawn dashed, by analogy to LTC4.
