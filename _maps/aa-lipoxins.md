Lipoxins need two lipoxygenases, often in two cells. In one route 15-lipoxygenase makes 15(S)-HpETE or 15(S)-HETE and leukocyte 5-lipoxygenase adds the second oxygen. In the other, platelet 12-lipoxygenase turns leukotriene A4 from neutrophils into lipoxins. Both routes pass through the epoxide 5,6-Ep-15(S)-HETE to lipoxin A4 and B4. Aspirin-acetylated COX-2 makes 15(R)-HpETE, which 5-lipoxygenase turns into the aspirin-triggered 15-epi-lipoxins. HPGD inactivates lipoxin A4. Human eosinophils conjugate the lipoxin epoxide with glutathione to lipoxin C4, which is trimmed to lipoxins D4 and E4. These were identified once, by their chromatography and light absorption. How lipoxin C4 leaves the cell is not known, and its export is drawn dashed, by analogy to LTC4.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for recovery. Lipoxin A4 production is lower in severe asthma than in moderate asthma, and the imbalance against the cysteinyl leukotrienes follows the loss of airflow, so failed resolution is part of the severity (Levy 2005).

In people, the cantharidin blister model shows the same from the other side: those whose inflammation resolves early have a rising 15-epi-lipoxin A4 response, and giving aspirin to slow resolvers raises it and lowers their leukocytes and cytokines (Morris 2010). The aspirin-triggered form also restores phagocytosis by neutrophils that have lost it and speeds the clearance of bacteria in lung injury, so these mediators actively help repair instead of only holding inflammation back (Sekheri 2020).
