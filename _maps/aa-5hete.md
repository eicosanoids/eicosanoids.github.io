5(S)-HpETE, made by 5-lipoxygenase with FLAP, is either turned into LTA4 or reduced to 5(S)-HETE by glutathione peroxidase activities, drawn here as MGST2 and MGST3. 5-hydroxyeicosanoid dehydrogenase, an enzyme known by its activity whose gene is not identified, oxidises 5(S)-HETE to 5-oxo-ETE, a strong chemoattractant for eosinophils. CYP4F2 and CYP4F3 hydroxylate 5(S)-HETE at carbon 20, and 12- and 15-lipoxygenase add a second oxygen to 5-oxo-ETE. The step from arachidonic acid to 5(S)-HpETE, drawn in grey, belongs to the LTB4 map.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. 5-Oxo-ETE, which this map makes from 5-HETE, is the strongest eicosanoid attractant for human eosinophils and works through its own receptor OXE (Powell 2013). An antagonist of that receptor blocked allergen-driven eosinophil entry and airway inflammation in sensitised monkeys, the first evidence in a living animal that this branch could be treated in eosinophilic asthma (Powell 2020). The step that makes it needs oxidative stress to run in intact cells, so this is a branch that opens under stress.
