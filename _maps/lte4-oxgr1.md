Leukotriene E4 is the long-lived cysteinyl leukotriene and the one that dominates in inflamed tissue, yet it has little potency at CysLT1 or CysLT2. It acts instead on GPR99, described before as an oxoglutarate receptor, which binds and responds to leukotriene E4 and is the candidate third cysteinyl leukotriene receptor (Kanaoka 2013). Mice without it lose the vascular leak that leukotriene E4 causes while keeping the response to leukotriene C4 and leukotriene D4. The G protein of this receptor is not established, so the map holds the binding step alone.
