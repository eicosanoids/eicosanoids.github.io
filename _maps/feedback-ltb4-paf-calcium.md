Cytosolic phospholipase A2 alpha needs calcium to reach its membrane substrate (Clark 1991). Cutting 1-alkyl-2-arachidonoyl phosphatidylcholine, it gives two mediators at once: arachidonic acid for the leukotrienes and lyso-PAF, which LPCAT2 acetylates to PAF (Shindou 2007). Mice without the enzyme make little of either (Uozumi 1997).

Both products come back to it. LTB4 on its receptor BLT1 and PAF on PTAFR raise calcium in the cell, so the enzyme is called again and more of both mediators is made. In neutrophils the LTB4 part of the loop relays a signal from cell to cell: cells that sense a formyl peptide or a dying cell make LTB4, and the LTB4 recruits and activates further neutrophils, which make more (Afonso 2012). In living tissue this relay builds the swarms of neutrophils around a site of cell death (Lämmermann 2013).

The map draws the rise in calcium as a stimulation of the step in which calcium binds the enzyme. The steps between, from the G protein through phospholipase C and inositol trisphosphate to the release of stored calcium, are in the receptor maps.
