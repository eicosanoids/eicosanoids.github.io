Secreted phospholipases release linoleic acid from membrane phospholipids. 15-lipoxygenase makes 13(S)-HpODE, which is reduced to 13(S)-HODE and oxidised to 13-oxo-ODE. Cyclooxygenases give mainly 9(R)-HODE, and free radicals give racemic 9- and 13-HODE (Hamberg 1998). Human platelet 12-lipoxygenase does not act on linoleic acid (Ikei 2012). CYP2C9 epoxidises it to the EpOMEs, known as leukotoxin and isoleukotoxin, and soluble epoxide hydrolase turns them into the DiHOMEs, which carry the toxicity (Moghaddam 1997). 15-lipoxygenase also makes the TriHOMEs in eosinophils, largely without epoxide hydrolase or P450 (Fuchs 2020). In the skin, 12R-lipoxygenase and eLOX3 oxidise linoleate while it is esterified in ω-O-acylceramide, and the epoxyalcohol ester is then hydrolysed to its triol by the epoxide hydrolases EPHX3 and soluble EPHX2, so the ceramide can be bound to the proteins of the barrier (Zheng 2011; Takeichi 2020; Yamanashi 2018; Edin 2021).
