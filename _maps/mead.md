Without the essential fatty acids the body makes mead acid (20:3 n-9), and 5-lipoxygenase turns it into LTA3 (Jakschik 1983). LTA3 is conjugated with glutathione to LTC3 and trimmed to LTD3 and LTE3, as active on smooth muscle as the 4-series (Hammarström 1981). LTB3 is not made. LTA3 blocks LTA4 hydrolase instead (Evans 1985), so mead acid lowers LTB4 production. Cytochrome P450 enzymes hydroxylate mead acid at its end and one carbon in (Fer 2008).

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. Mead acid is a marker rather than a mediator: it is made from oleate by the same desaturases and elongase as arachidonic acid, which is why it appears only when linoleate is missing (Ichi 2014). The marker is not read off mead acid alone. Patients on an olive-oil based lipid emulsion had raised mead acid and low linoleate while their triene to tetraene ratio stayed well below the threshold for deficiency (Gramlich 2015), and a paediatric trial of such an emulsion found no essential fatty acid deficiency although mead acid rose, with desaturase variants changing the picture (Blanco 2025).
