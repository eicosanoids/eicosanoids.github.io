Free radicals oxidise arachidonic acid without enzymes, mostly while it is still esterified in phospholipids, and give the F2-isoprostanes, among them 8-iso-PGF2α and 8,12-iso-iPF2α-VI. β-oxidation shortens 8-iso-PGF2α by two carbons to 2,3-dinor-8-iso-PGF2α and to 2,3-dinor-8-iso-PGF1α (also named 2,3-dinor-5,6-dihydro-8-iso-PGF2α), which has lost the 5,6 double bond as well. Both are excreted in urine in amounts at least as large as 8-iso-PGF2α itself and are measured as markers of oxidative stress.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. These products are made without an enzyme, and 8-isoprostane in urine is the most widely used marker of oxidative stress in the body, rising with body mass index in healthy adults (Graille 2020).

They are not only markers. 8-epi-PGF2alpha constricts coronary arteries as a partial agonist at the thromboxane receptor (Kromer 1996). Where that leads is unsettled: in mice lacking the LDL receptor, infused 8-iso-PGF2alpha tended to reduce atherosclerotic lesions, while deleting the thromboxane receptor in smooth muscle protected, so the part this axis plays in atherosclerosis is open (Braun 2022).
