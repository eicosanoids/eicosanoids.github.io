A prostaglandin outside the cell is out of reach of the enzyme that ends its signal. The transporter SLCO2A1 brings it back in, and 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase then oxidises carbon 15 in the cytoplasm. Cells make the oxidised product only when both proteins are present, and the metabolite then leaves again by diffusion (Nomura 2004).

Both halves of the pair are known from human disease, and they fail in opposite ways. People without the transporter have raised PGE2 in urine but still excrete the shortened metabolite PGE-M, while people without the dehydrogenase have raised PGE2 and little PGE-M. Both conditions give the same bone and skin disease, primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy. Mice without the transporter fail to close the ductus arteriosus after birth (Chang 2010).

The reductase PTGR1 then saturates the double bond. The same enzyme inactivates lipoxin A4, so it is a shared node between the prostanoid and the lipoxin route.

## What this map means

These are the steps a mediator takes after it has acted, the routes by which it leaves the network. They are not new branches of production. Where a product keeps its activity, this text says so, because the route alone does not tell whether a signal has ended.
