PGD2 loses water without an enzyme and becomes 15-deoxy-delta12,14-PGJ2, a ligand of the nuclear receptor PPARγ. In macrophage-like U937 cells 15d-PGJ2 lowers the expression of cyclooxygenase-2 that lipopolysaccharide turns on, by getting in the way of NF-κB on the PTGS2 promoter, and endothelial cells, which have little PPARγ, gain the effect when they are given the receptor (Inoue 2000). A product of COX-2 would then hold back COX-2, a negative loop.

The loop is contested and drawn green. 15d-PGJ2 is a minor product and does not rise where PPARγ activation is claimed, so it may never reach the concentrations this brake needs in the body (Bell-Parikh 2003). The step from PGD2 to 15d-PGJ2 is drawn as one here. The PGD2 map gives its intermediates.
