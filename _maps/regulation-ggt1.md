Gamma-glutamyl transpeptidase takes the glutamate off leukotriene C4 and so makes leukotriene D4. The enzyme is made as one chain that can do nothing, and it becomes active by cutting itself in two. The cut frees threonine 381 as the nucleophile of the active site, and the two pieces stay together as the working enzyme (West 2011, Terzyan 2015).

The cut needs a sugar. Of the seven sites that carry N-glycans, the one on asparagine 95 is the one that matters: without it the enzyme hardly cuts itself and the inactive single chain piles up, and removing all seven leaves nothing but that chain. Once the enzyme is mature the glycans no longer matter, for its kinetics or for how well it holds together (West 2011). So a sugar decides whether an enzyme is ever made, and then stops mattering.
