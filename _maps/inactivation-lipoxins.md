Two enzymes do almost all of it, and one of them does three different things. 15-Hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase oxidises carbon 15 of lipoxin A4, carbon 5 of lipoxin B4, and then runs backwards as a reductase on the saturated intermediate (Clish 2000, Maddox 1998). Between the two oxidations PTGR1 saturates the double bond. Both products of the lipoxin A4 route are inactive in three assays, so here the route and the outcome agree.

Carbon 20 is hydroxylated by CYP4F3, the same enzyme that does it for leukotriene B4, with a much higher Michaelis constant, so leukotriene B4 wins when both are present (Sumimoto 1993, Kikuta 1998).

Two steps are drawn green. The omega-oxidation of lipoxin B4 is real, but its products are as active as lipoxin B4 itself for monocyte adherence, so it is not an inactivation (Maddox 1996). And the aspirin-triggered epimer 15-epi-lipoxin A4, often called resistant to this route, is oxidised to the same 15-oxo product in human stromal cells, and inhibitors of the dehydrogenase cut that conversion (Dakin 2017). What is actually established is that the enzyme prefers the 15(S) alcohol and that synthetic analogues protected at carbon 15 are stable.

Worth a note for anyone reading this as pharmacology: diclofenac and indomethacin inhibit PTGR1, so some anti-inflammatory drugs slow the breakdown of lipoxins as well as blocking cyclooxygenase (Clish 2001).

## What this map means

These are the steps a mediator takes after it has acted, the routes by which it leaves the network. They are not new branches of production. Where a product keeps its activity, this text says so, because the route alone does not tell whether a signal has ended.
