15-lipoxygenase turns docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) into 17S-HpDHA (human ALOX15 makes it together with 14S-HpDHA, about one to one; ALOX15B makes 17S-HpDHA almost alone, Kutzner 2017), and 5-lipoxygenase in leukocytes adds a second oxygen at carbon 7 or 4. The 7-hydroperoxide is reduced to resolvin D5 or turned into a 7S(8)-epoxide that opens to resolvin D1 and D2, and the 4-hydroperoxide gives the 4S(5)-epoxide behind resolvins D3 and D4 (Serhan 2002; Sun 2007). Aspirin-acetylated COX-2 makes 17R-HpDHA, the start of the aspirin-triggered resolvins. The 4-hydroperoxide also gives resolvin D6 directly. Resolvin D1 is inactivated by oxidation at carbon 8 or 17. The epoxide is conjugated with glutathione to the RCTR series, and the aspirin-triggered route gives AT-resolvin D3 as well. How much of the epoxide route runs in human leukocytes is debated: human 5-lipoxygenase acts slowly on 17S-HpDHA, and the dihydroxy product resolvin D5 is found more consistently than the trihydroxy resolvins (Perry 2020; Kahnt 2023).

One predicted step is drawn with a dashed arrow: the oxidation of resolvin D2, by analogy to resolvins D1 and E1, which 15-hydroxyprostaglandin dehydrogenase inactivates.

The route through the 4-hydroperoxide is drawn in green as contested: recombinant human 5-lipoxygenase makes no 4S-HDHA from DHA, 4-HDHA is a main product of DHA autoxidation, and resolvins D3 and D4 have been seen only when the synthetic 4S,5S-epoxide was added to leukocytes (Kahnt 2023; Schebb 2026; Shay 2021). The green steps are the 4-hydroperoxide, its epoxide, resolvins D3, D4 and D6, and AT-resolvin D3. The 7S(8)-epoxide route to resolvins D1 and D2 stays solid: human neutrophils make it when 17-HDHA is added, through FLAP, in small amounts beside resolvin D5 (Mainka 2022).
