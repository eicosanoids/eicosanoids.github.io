Cytochrome P450 epoxygenases, mainly CYP2J2 and CYP2C enzymes, add an epoxide to any of the four double bonds of arachidonic acid, giving 5,6-, 8,9-, 11,12- and 14,15-EET. The EETs dilate vessels and limit inflammation. Soluble epoxide hydrolase (EPHX2) turns them into the less active diols, the DHETs, so its inhibitors raise EET levels.

## Relevance to disease

Where to look at this pathway from, for disease. The epoxides of this map are kept alive longer when soluble epoxide hydrolase is blocked, and the inhibitor EC5026 passed a first trial in people as a painkiller that is neither an opioid nor an anti-inflammatory drug (Schmidt 2024). In the heart the same approach attenuated the progression of heart failure after myocardial infarction in normotensive rats but not in hypertensive ones, so the protection is real and depends on the background (Hrdlicka 2019). The map draws the hydrolysis step for that reason: in coronary endothelium it is the smaller fate of these epoxides, behind the shortening route.
